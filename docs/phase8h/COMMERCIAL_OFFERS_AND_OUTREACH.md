**All prices are pricing hypotheses awaiting owner approval. No customer, case study, testimonial, partnership or result is claimed anywhere in this document.** Nothing here is sent, published or contacted. Every outreach message, every list of target accounts and every price requires the owner's explicit approval before use. Templates contain placeholders in `[square brackets]` that must be filled with facts the owner can verify.

## 1. Why these three offers first
Ten services exist on `services.php`. For the first customer the aim is the **shortest path to a signed contract with real cash**, so each service was scored (1 to 5, my judgement, to be challenged) on five criteria: deal size, time to cash, delivery risk (higher score means lower risk), differentiation, and how well it leads to a recurring contract.

| **AI readiness and roadmap sprint** (new framing of discovery) | 2 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | **19** |
| **AI workflow automation and agent sprint** (services 1 and 4) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **20** |

The top three form a **ladder**: a small, low-risk entry (readiness sprint), then a build (automation or agent sprint, or a knowledge assistant pilot), then a **managed retainer**. Custom SaaS and modernisation are valuable later, once references exist.

## 2. The three offers

### Offer 1. AI readiness and roadmap sprint (the entry offer)
- **Buyer:** operations, finance, customer-service, legal or IT leader at a company with repetitive, document-heavy or rules-driven work and some budget authority.
- **Problem:** "We know we should use AI but do not know where it pays back, what is safe, or who should build it."
- **Scope (2 weeks, fixed scope):** interviews with up to 5 people; map 3 to 5 candidate workflows; score each on value, data readiness, risk and effort; a data and access review; a risk and human-oversight note (including EU AI Act classification questions to confirm with the client's counsel); a ranked 6-month roadmap with a recommended first build and a fixed-price proposal for it.
- **Deliverables:** a written report, a one-page executive summary, a scored backlog, and a recorded readout.
- **Not included:** implementation, legal advice, guarantees of savings. Outcomes are estimates with stated assumptions.
- **Pricing hypothesis (unapproved):** fixed **EUR 4,900** (range 3,900 to 7,500), 50% on signature, 50% on delivery; credited against a follow-on build if signed within 30 days (an incentive to convert, to be approved).
- **Why it sells:** low commitment, quick decision, no integration risk, and it creates a qualified build proposal.
- **Delivery risk:** low; mainly time and structured interviewing.

### Offer 2. AI workflow automation and agent sprint
- **Buyer:** the same leaders, with one clearly bounded workflow (for example inbound request triage, document intake and routing, report preparation, data enrichment with human approval).
- **Problem:** "A repetitive process eats expensive hours and is error-prone; we want it automated safely with a person in control."
- **Scope (4 to 8 weeks, fixed scope for ONE workflow):** requirements and acceptance tests agreed in writing; a working agent or automation integrated with up to 3 of the client's systems; guardrails (approval steps, limits, logging); an evaluation set to measure accuracy before go-live; monitoring and a runbook; training for the owner of the process; a 30-day stabilisation period.
- **Deliverables:** deployed solution in the client's environment, source and documentation, evaluation report, handover.
- **Not included:** open-ended scope, work on more than one workflow, uncapped usage costs of third-party AI services (passed through at cost).
- **Pricing hypothesis (unapproved):** fixed **EUR 18,000 to 32,000** depending on integrations and risk; 40% on signature, 30% at a mid-point demo, 30% at acceptance. Optional **managed AI operations retainer** after go-live: **EUR 2,500 to 6,000 per month** (monitoring, tuning, incident response, small changes within an agreed allowance).
- **Delivery risk:** medium; mitigated by one workflow, written acceptance tests and a measured evaluation.

### Offer 3. Enterprise knowledge assistant pilot (document intelligence)
- **Buyer:** organisations with manuals, policies, contracts or support knowledge that staff or customers cannot search effectively.
- **Problem:** "People cannot find reliable answers in our documents, and we cannot risk an assistant that makes things up."
- **Scope (6 to 8 weeks, fixed scope, up to an agreed document set):** ingestion and permission mapping; retrieval and answer generation that **cites the source passage** and says when it does not know; access control matching existing rules; an evaluation set built with the client's experts; a pilot with a defined user group; cost and quality report; production readiness plan.
- **Pricing hypothesis (unapproved):** fixed **EUR 24,000 to 42,000** for the pilot; **retainer EUR 3,500 to 8,000 per month** for operation and improvement.
- **Delivery risk:** medium; data quality and permissions are the main risks, so the sprint starts with a data review and a go or no-go.

## 3. Packaging rules (apply to all offers)
1. Fixed scope and fixed price per phase; change requests are priced separately.
2. Written acceptance criteria; payment tied to milestones.
3. Honest-claims rule (already in the services pages): no promised outcomes, AI systems can be wrong, human review is built in.
4. Client data handling: a data-processing agreement, least-privilege access, no client data used to train third-party models without written consent.
5. Contract documents prepared with legal counsel before the first signature (decision needed): master services agreement, statement of work, data-processing agreement, liability cap, IP assignment, confidentiality.
6. Proof assets (no fake references): a **sample redacted readiness report**, an **architecture one-pager**, an **evaluation method one-pager**, and **a working demo built on public data** (for example a tool-selection assistant over the AIHubpedia catalogue). Case studies appear only after a client gives written permission.

## 4. Prospect qualification criteria
Score each target account 0 to 100; approach accounts scoring 60 or more first; any "stop" item removes the account.
| Criterion | Points | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|
| Named role with budget influence for operations, IT or customer service | 20 | Public profile or company page |
| A visible repetitive, document-heavy or rules-driven process | 20 | Job posts, product pages, public statements |
| Company size where a EUR 15,000 to 40,000 project is a normal purchase (the owner sets the range) | 15 | Public size information |
| Existing digital maturity (cloud, APIs, modern tools) | 10 | Public tech clues |
| Regulatory or data-sensitivity that makes careful delivery a differentiator | 10 | Sector |
| Language and time-zone fit with delivery capacity | 10 | Location |
| Signs of AI interest (talks, posts, hiring) | 10 | Public sources |
| Warm path (shared contact, partner, community) | 5 | Record the path |
**Stop items:** public notice that they do not want unsolicited vendor approaches; sanctioned or restricted entities; a conflict with an existing client; an individual without a business relevance.
Record each account in the **CRM** (company, role, source, score, next step, consent basis). No personal data beyond business contact details found on public business sources.

## 5. Discovery call script (30 minutes)
1. **Open (2 min):** who we are, the purpose, how long, permission to take notes.
2. **Situation (8 min):** "Walk me through the process that costs you the most time. Who is involved? How often? What goes wrong?"
3. **Impact (5 min):** "How is it measured today? What would success look like in 90 days?" (record the client's own numbers; never supply numbers.)
4. **Constraints (5 min):** data location and sensitivity, systems, approvals, compliance, who signs.
5. **Fit (5 min):** explain the readiness sprint and the build option in two sentences each; ask which feels closer.
6. **Next step (5 min):** agree a date for a written proposal or a scoping workshop. Send a summary in the next hour.
Disqualify politely when there is no budget owner, no real process or unrealistic expectations of guaranteed outcomes.

## 6. Proposal outline (use the CRM proposal feature)
Context in the client's words; objective and success measures agreed with them; scope in and out; deliverables; approach and timeline; roles and client responsibilities; acceptance criteria; price and payment milestones; assumptions and risks; data protection; terms; validity date. Each version is stored and must be approved by the owner before it is sent.

## 7. Outreach rules (non-negotiable)
- **No contact without the owner's approval of the exact account list and the exact message.** Nothing is sent automatically.
- Send only to **business** contacts, identified from public business sources, with a clear sender identity, a real postal or company identity, and a **simple opt-out**. Honour opt-outs and do-not-contact lists permanently. Follow GDPR, the national rules on unsolicited business communication in each target country, and CAN-SPAM for US recipients; confirm the lawful basis (legitimate interest for relevant B2B approaches) and the retention period with counsel before the first send.
- Personalise with **verifiable public facts** only. No false claims, no fake urgency, no invented clients, partners, results or endorsements. Do not claim traffic, rankings or audience that have not been measured.
- Maximum of two follow-ups per account, spaced at least a week apart, then stop.
- Use a dedicated sending domain with correct SPF, DKIM and DMARC (decision needed); no purchased lists; no scraping of personal email addresses.
- Log every approach in the CRM so replies, opt-outs and outcomes are measured.

## 9. Objection handling (honest answers)
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Do you have references?" | "Not published yet. We reduce your risk with a small fixed-scope first step, written acceptance tests and milestone payments. We can share our method and a sample report." |
| "AI makes things up." | "Correct, which is why every build has an evaluation set, a source-citing design where relevant, human approval points and monitoring." |
| "Why not do it in-house?" | "You may. The readiness sprint shows what to do in-house and what to buy." |
| "Too expensive." | "Start with the sprint; the build is optional and priced against measured value." |
| "Data security?" | "Least-privilege access, a data-processing agreement, no use of your data to train third-party models without written consent, logging and a defined retention period." |

## 10. Approval workflow for any commercial action
1. Owner approves: the offer text, the price, the target account list (with scores), the template and the sending identity.
2. I log the approved batch in the CRM before anything is sent.
3. The owner (or a person the owner names) sends; I do not send.
4. Replies are logged; the owner decides every proposal and price before it leaves.
5. Weekly review of the funnel with measured rates; assumptions in `FINANCIAL_MODEL.md` are updated from real numbers.
