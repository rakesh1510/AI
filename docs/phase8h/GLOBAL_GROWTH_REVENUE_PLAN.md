## 1. The CEO position in one page
1. **The goal is EUR 979,000 of revenue per month. Today the measured revenue is nothing we can see.** No customer, contract, visitor figure or payment exists in the repository, and analytics tracking has never been switched on. Any plan that skips this fact is fiction.
2. **Sell services first.** One signed TrustNova Tech AI project outweighs a year of advertising revenue at any audience we could build soon, and it needs no audience. Advertising and vendor revenue follow audience and trust; they cannot lead.
3. **The model says the constraint is the first customer and deal size, then capacity.** The target needs roughly 120 active accounts at EUR 8,000 per month, or about 40 accounts at EUR 25,000 per month, and 45 to 65 delivery people. We win by aiming at accounts that can grow and by productising delivery, not by many small jobs.
4. **Stop building admin features.** Phases 8A to 8D give us a CRM, proposals, a Finance module and a target ladder. The next software work only matters if it removes friction between a stranger and a signed contract (landing pages, booking, proposal speed, measurement).
5. **Everything that touches a customer, a price, a message or money needs the owner's approval.** This plan prepares; it does not act.

## 3. Strategy pillars
### Pillar 1. The first enterprise customer (weeks 1 to 12)
- **Offer ladder** (see the offers document): AI readiness sprint (entry), AI workflow automation and agent sprint, enterprise knowledge assistant pilot, then a managed retainer. Pricing hypotheses are in the offers document and are not approved.
- **Targeting:** build a list of 150 to 250 accounts scored by the qualification criteria, focused on one or two sectors where the owner has credibility and where repetitive document or workflow pain is visible (for example professional services, logistics, insurance and finance operations, healthcare administration, e-commerce operations). The owner chooses the sectors and regions.
- **Channels (all after approval):** personalised direct outreach to business contacts; warm introductions; partner referrals; the inbound form; content that proves method (see pillar 4).
- **Proof without invention:** a sample redacted readiness report, an evaluation-method one-pager, an architecture one-pager and a live demo built on public data. Case studies only with written client permission.
- **Funnel arithmetic:** about 150 to 250 well-chosen accounts for the first win at hypothetical rates (see the model). Measure the real rates from the first 50 accounts and update.

### Pillar 2. Recurring contracts
Every build ends with a **managed AI operations retainer** (monitoring, tuning, incident response, small changes within an allowance). Target at least half of the projects converting to a retainer. Retainers raise ARPA and lower churn risk; they also smooth cash. Billing monthly in advance reduces the funding gap shown in the model.

### Pillar 3. International B2B partnerships
- **Partner types:** digital and automation agencies without AI delivery depth; cloud and systems integrators; software vendors whose customers need implementation; AI vendors that need integration capacity.
- **Value exchange:** we offer delivery capacity and a vetted method; partners offer introductions and trust. Starting terms to propose (hypotheses): referral fee of 10 to 15% of first-year fees for introductions that sign, or a co-delivery split agreed per project. No exclusivity. A written partner agreement and a data-processing arrangement are required.
- **Regions to evaluate first (hypothesis, verify with demand and capacity):** DACH, Benelux, Nordics, UK and Ireland, because of time-zone overlap, English or German delivery, and demand for compliant AI. The EU AI Act creates a service opportunity (classification, documentation, human oversight); confirm current obligations and timelines with legal counsel before marketing any compliance claim.
- **Sequence:** 10 partner conversations in the first 90 days, aiming for 2 active relationships.

### Pillar 4. Organic traffic and global recognition (a long game)
- **Original research from our own evidence base:** pricing and plan changes tracked over time, a verified model and release timeline from the AI Universe, evidence-backed comparisons. These are linkable assets that nobody can copy overnight.
- **Authority pages:** category and comparison pages that show sources and verification state; industry guides written from evidence (for the same sectors we target for services, so content and sales reinforce each other).
- **International:** start with English; add languages by **measured search demand**, not guesses (needs Search Console data once live). The `hreflang` architecture exists.
- **Editorial and community relationships:** newsletters, communities and industry publications, by contribution and not spam. No fake traffic, no purchased links, no unverified claims.
- **Honest expectations:** organic traffic compounds over months. It does not fund the first year.

### Pillar 5. AIHubpedia advertisers and vendors (gated)
Pilot with two or three vendors only after (a) at least three consecutive months of measured data and (b) verified profiles to show, with prices set by the owner. Placements stay clearly labelled and never change a rating or ranking. Newsletter sponsorship stays blocked until a newsletter exists with measured clicks. No audience figure is quoted until measured.

### Pillar 6. Competitive differentiation
- **For users:** evidence with sources and verification state, independence from sponsorship, and honest "unavailable" states where others invent numbers.
- **For enterprises:** the same people who evaluate AI tools build AI systems, and the readiness sprint uses that evidence base to recommend what to buy and what to build.
- **What competitors have and we lack today:** audience, brand, backlinks and references. We win by being more trustworthy and more useful in a narrow area first, then widening. Verify any competitor figure before using it; none is used in this plan.

## 4. High-conversion landing pages (design brief, no code yet)
Current state: ten equal service cards and a long guided inquiry form. Recommended changes for approval:
1. **Three offer pages** (readiness sprint, automation and agent sprint, knowledge assistant pilot), each with one purpose and one primary action: "Book a 30-minute scoping call".
2. **Above the fold:** the buyer's problem in their words, what they get, how long, and what happens next. Prices appear as "from EUR X" only if the owner approves publishing them; otherwise "fixed price after a short call".
3. **Trust, truthfully:** method, fixed scope and milestone payments, sample deliverables, data-handling commitments, who delivers, and FAQ. No logos, testimonials or numbers we cannot prove.
4. **Low-friction booking:** a short form (company, role, one-sentence problem, preferred time) before the long guided form; reply within one business day (a named person).
5. **Measurement:** attribution by source and offer through the CRM; page analytics only after the owner approves tracking.
6. **Experiments (sequential, because traffic is small):** headline, call-to-action wording, form length, price shown or hidden; judged on qualified inquiries per visitor, not clicks.
7. **Performance and accessibility:** keep the existing standards (fast, accessible, mobile first).

## 7. Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| No references, so enterprises hesitate | Small fixed-scope first step, milestones, sample deliverables, honest method; permissioned case study after the first win |
| Delivery capacity (owner is the constraint) | Subcontractor bench, one workflow per sprint, productised templates, no work beyond capacity |
| A project fails or an AI system errs | Written acceptance tests, evaluation sets, human oversight, a liability cap in contracts, insurance reviewed with counsel |
| Legal and data-protection exposure | Counsel-reviewed contracts and DPA; EU AI Act questions handled with counsel; no data used for training without consent |
| Outreach damages reputation or breaks rules | Approved lists and templates only, opt-out always honoured, authenticated sending domain, no purchased lists |
| Cash gap in the first year | Upfront payments, monthly billing in advance, project-based costs; no outside capital assumed |
| Customer concentration | Several accounts; the Finance module shows the largest contract's share |
| Distraction into software features | Stop rule: no new admin feature unless it removes a measured obstacle to revenue |
| The conservative case is the likeliest | Plan decisions so they still make sense if revenue is slow |
